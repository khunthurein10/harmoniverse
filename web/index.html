Two satellites see differently

MODIS (since 2000) has 1 km pixels. VIIRS (since 2012) has 375 m pixels and catches many small fires MODIS misses. MODIS is now retiring, so the future record is VIIRS.

Chapter 03

One common language

Joining the records naively makes worldwide fire look about 6 times higher once VIIRS arrives (+478%), while the real change was −3.7%. For the same fires in 2012–2015, S-NPP VIIRS counted 6.0× more hotspots than Aqua MODIS. We convert the MODIS years into what S-NPP VIIRS would have seen, so the whole record is on one scale.

FAQ · before you explore

Common questions,
clear answers.

A quick guide to the data, the calendar, and what to keep in mind as you explore.

Where does the fire data come from?

From NASA FIRMS active fire detections: MODIS Collection 6.1 (1 km, since 2000) and VIIRS (375 m, since 2012). Up to January 2012 the counts are converted from MODIS; from February 2012 they are real S-NPP VIIRS counts. The record runs from January 2003 to October 2026.

Why harmonize MODIS and VIIRS?

VIIRS sees many small fires that MODIS misses, so raw counts jump when it arrives in 2012. We convert the MODIS years to the S-NPP VIIRS scale, using years when both satellites flew to train and test the conversion, so long-term comparisons stay fair.

Does one hotspot mean one fire?

No. One large fire can produce hundreds of hotspots across days and satellite overpasses. Read counts as fire activity, not a number of fires.

How do I read the burning calendar?

Each row is a year and each column a month. Color shows how many hotspots that month had, and a white dot marks months above the 90th percentile for that month.

Can I use this for emergency decisions?

No. Harmoniverse describes past activity for planning and research. For active fires, follow local authorities and official alert systems.

The number of S-NPP hotspots that one Aqua hotspot stands for.

Step 3

Multiply

Aqua count × ratio_used ≈ the count on the S-NPP VIIRS scale.

Example: Australia and New Zealand, day, January has ratio_used 5.41. So 100 Aqua hotspots ≈ 541 S-NPP hotspots.

Where it fits

The record’s main conversion (2003 to January 2012) is a gradient boosting model. This table is its simple, explainable baseline, and the fallback when a month is bigger than anything the model learned from.

On the untouched test years 2012–2014, the table missed regional monthly totals by 7.2% and the model by 5.4% (fire-weighted, final test). The model is more accurate; the table is easy to check by hand.

Before you download

This table is not our harmonization

The lookup table is a simple ratio: S-NPP hotspots per Aqua hotspot, for each region, pass and month. It is the explainable baseline and the fallback, nothing more.

  • The harmonized record uses a model. Two gradient boosting models (day and night) convert every MODIS month, using Aqua count, fire-days, fire power and pixel size, Terra count, region, land type, month and location.
  • The model is more accurate. On the untouched test years 2012–2014: regional monthly error 5.4% for the model, 7.2% for this table.
  • The table is used only when a month is bigger than anything the model learned from (flagged above_training_range in the data).

Multiplying MODIS counts by these ratios will not reproduce our harmonized numbers.
